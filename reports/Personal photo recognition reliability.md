# Eymra must verify people before ranking

Eymra contains reproducible application bugs that can turn a correctly rejected friend, or a person whose face is invisible, into a photo labelled “You.” It also allows a correctly classified friend to win a personal single-photo selection because identity is only a small ranking bonus. **These failures exist independently of whether the face model is accurate.** The app already uses a dedicated SFace recognition model and an optional multimodal judge; simply adding another language model would leave the deterministic failures intact. The immediate remedy is to preserve identity uncertainty, require identity eligibility for personal selections, and prevent predictions from becoming their own supporting evidence. Model quality still needs investigation: this audit found face-association and enrollment risks, and the existing benchmarks do not establish reliability on the owner's library. The actual failing photos, installed binary and saved face references were not inspected, so the code defects are established but the cause of each reported example remains unverified.

Research date: October 26, 2026. Code findings below describe the audited baseline. The accompanying fixes have been implemented and Simulator validation completed; their scope and validation boundary are stated at the end.

## The application can manufacture identity after recognition fails

The most serious defect appears after recognition finishes. When no photo confidently matches the enrolled owner, `CurationPipeline` scores the collection again with `me: nil`. That replacement discards the original identity decisions and the uncertain faces that could have prompted review. Without a profile, `CategoryRules` treats framing as identity: a sufficiently large person becomes `.me`, even with **zero detected faces**. The interface displays this category as “You.” A back-facing body therefore needs no mistaken biometric match to receive the wrong label. A readable friend whose signature correctly fails comparison follows the same route. An existing fixture test explicitly expected this fallback, which explains why a passing test suite could preserve the defect. ([Pipeline](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Pipeline/CurationPipeline.swift), [category rules](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/CategoryRules.swift), [fixture tests](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Tests/EymraTests/PipelineFixtureTests.swift))

Selection has a separate problem. The audited selector admits every candidate, then adds small bonuses for the owner. In a single-photo run with “More of me” and “Mostly me” preferences, a recognized-owner photo with relevance 0.4 scores 0.58 before any equal scene bonus; a known-friend photo with relevance 1.0 scores 0.63. The friend wins despite correct recognition. This is a direct calculation from the weights, not an estimate of real-world error frequency. **A requirement about whose photograph to post cannot be guaranteed by a quality bonus.** Personal selection must establish the eligible set first, then optimize quality within it. If no photo qualifies, the honest result is a shortfall with review or enrollment options. A mixed travel story can have a different contract, but the product must distinguish that from a personal post. ([Selector](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Selection.swift), [weights](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Tuning.swift))

The audited reference expansion creates another unsupported promotion path. Faces scoring at least 0.55 against enrollment join the run's reference bank; other faces are then accepted at 0.50 against the expanded bank. Consider normalized vectors: enrollment p=(1,0), added reference a=(0.6,0.8), and query b=(0.3,√0.91). The query scores 0.3 against enrollment but about 0.943 against a. Thus a rejected query can become a confident match through an unconfirmed prediction. Restricting additions to direct enrollment matches prevents endless recursion, but does not prevent this single-step contamination. Removing expansion makes a fixed photo's identity independent of which unrelated photos are included in the date range. ([Identity implementation](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Identity.swift), [scoring](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Scoring.swift))

Unknown also needs to survive at the photo level. Previously, one readable friend could make an entire group “not you,” even if another face was unreadable or beyond the six-face embedding limit. Grouping can compound the problem: missing signatures and groups of three or more bypass identity checks, and quality chooses cluster winners before personal selection. A better friend photo can suppress the only owner-containing frame before it reaches the selector. Eligibility must therefore survive grouping, judge promotion, reranking and swaps, rather than being checked only at the final step. ([Clustering](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Clustering.swift), [judge stage](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Pipeline/JudgeStage.swift))

## Face verification needs observable faces and correct associations

The current chain is Apple Vision face detection, a sharper crop, five facial landmarks, alignment to 112×112 pixels, Core ML SFace inference, normalization to a 128-number vector, and cosine comparison against enrollment. These are distinct jobs. Detection answers where a face might be; alignment standardizes its geometry; embedding encodes features useful for comparing identity. A detector finding a person or a network returning a vector does not by itself establish who that person is. Source inspection found no obvious universal RGB or input-scale mismatch: the documented conversion agrees with OpenCV's reference preprocessing and template. That is useful evidence, but does not replace numerical parity testing of the exact conversion. ([Face embedder](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Services/FaceEmbedder.swift), [conversion notes](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/ThirdParty/README.md), [OpenCV implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/objdetect/src/face_recognize.cpp))

A concrete association gap remains in the audited extraction path. It expands a face crop to 2.2 times the face size, re-detects faces, and takes whichever is closest to the crop centre. It does not verify that the new detection corresponds to the original face. At an image edge the crop centre shifts; in a group the crop can contain a neighbor. If the intended face is missed, the neighbor can supply its signature to the original face's record. Preserve the expected rectangle through cropping and resizing, match re-detections against that geometry, and abstain when the association is absent or ambiguous. The code establishes the missing check; only replaying real examples can establish its incidence. ([Face embedder](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Services/FaceEmbedder.swift))

Pose, quality and landmark consistency also need evaluation. Runtime identity admission mostly requires size and the existence of landmarks. Capture quality affects aesthetic scoring, but does not currently express whether identity is readable. The landmark adapter averages the nose outline even though the template represents a nose-tip location; Apple's outline documentation makes this a semantic mismatch worth measuring, not sufficient evidence of the dominant error. Any pose or quality gates should be calibrated rather than chosen to make a few examples pass. ([Apple nose landmarks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfacelandmarks2d/nose?language=objc), [NIST face-quality evaluation](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt_quality.html))

**A fully hidden face supplies no facial evidence.** Clothing, body shape, time, location and nearby frontal photos can support contextual person recognition, but this is a separate problem. Research on personal photo collections uses head, body and context precisely because faces can be invisible; clothing changes and shared clothing create difficult cases. The immediate trustworthy behavior for a back view is unknown plus photo-specific confirmation. That confirmation must not become a supposed face embedding or a permanent rule that anyone wearing similar clothes is the owner. Event-local linking is a later feature requiring separate evaluation. ([Oh et al., person recognition in personal photo collections](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.03224v2))

## A language model can judge composition without deciding identity

Eymra already asks a multimodal judge about sharpness, exposure, composition, subject and moment. Its prompt prohibits guessing identities, but uses `.me` as shorthand for a person filling the frame. That vocabulary mixes composition with identity and should become identity-neutral concepts such as portrait and environmental portrait. The authoritative owner-presence field should remain separate. Claude's documentation describes image limitations, including small or rotated images, and does not establish calibrated biometric verification capability. A persuasive explanation or an asserted confidence percentage is not an identity measurement. ([Judge prompt](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Services/AIJudge/JudgePrompt.swift), [Claude vision documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision))

For eligible photos, stronger photographic assessment is reasonable. The current composition heuristic rewards saliency surviving a crop, placement near thirds or centre lines, and a preferred subject area. It does not explicitly protect the recognized owner's face or body. A target-aware scorer should check expression, face clarity, lighting, headroom, body crop and distractions for the intended person. Apple's capture-quality signal can help compare that person's captures; whole-image aesthetics can help rank visual presentation. Neither substitutes for identity. A multimodal judge can compare a small eligible shortlist for visible photographic differences, with order randomized and ties allowed. Its benefit must be measured against the owner's choices. ([Scoring](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Scoring.swift), [Apple selfie quality](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/selecting-a-selfie-based-on-capture-quality), [Apple aesthetics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/calculateimageaestheticsscoresrequest))

Story selection follows those checks. An opener, distinct moments, contextual views and a closer are set-level objectives. They should not override who must appear in a personal post. Human photographic preference is also subjective: NIMA models a distribution of quality ratings rather than assuming one objectively correct score. The right evaluation includes preference comparisons, not only technical sharpness or a generic aesthetic score. ([NIMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05424))

Model replacement should remain an evidence-driven experiment. Keep SFace as the reproducible baseline. EdgeFace is worth a controlled device benchmark: its author repository provides pretrained checkpoints and a BSD-3-Clause license, contradicting the older decision log's blanket commercial-use dismissal. Verify the exact checkpoint's distribution terms before shipping. InsightFace distinguishes MIT code from non-commercial pretrained model packs; commercially licensed weights require separate consideration. AuraFace publishes an Apache-2.0 ResNet100 model, but this audit establishes no iPhone latency or personal-library advantage. Any replacement requires conversion parity, fresh thresholds and new enrollment embeddings; equal vector dimensions do not make two models compatible. ([EdgeFace repository](https://github.com/otroshi/edgeface), [EdgeFace license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/otroshi/edgeface/main/LICENSE), [InsightFace licensing](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface/blob/master/README.md?plain=1), [AuraFace model card](https://huggingface.co/fal/AuraFace-v1))

## Validation must measure wrong-person picks in real collections

The historical “98–99%” claims cannot establish this product's reliability. Local model tests compare pairs at threshold 0.40, while production uses multiple references with acceptance at 0.50 and uncertainty beginning at 0.38. Unread pairs are skipped, and missing local fixtures can return without evaluation. The small-face test restores an intact original image after detecting on a downsample; it tests recovery from rendition resizing, not a face that was intrinsically tiny in the source photograph. OpenCV itself reports different SFace operating thresholds across datasets. A benchmark threshold is not a universal probability of correctness. ([Recognition tests](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Tests/EymraTests/FaceRecognitionTests.swift), [OpenCV tutorial](https://docs.opencv.org/4.5.5/d0/dd4/tutorial_dnn_face.html))

The next empirical step is a consented, local collection containing actual failures and representative successes: owner alone, frequent friends and look-alikes, groups, side and rear views, low light, blur, glasses, tiny faces and people posing at the same location. Separate enrollment, calibration and final evaluation by event or day so neighboring burst frames cannot leak across splits. Measure detection coverage, association errors, unreadability, false acceptance and false rejection separately. Then measure the product outcome: **wrong-person rate among final picks, personal top-one correctness, unknown rate, crop preservation and preference win rate**. Report unread images in the denominator appropriate to the end-to-end task instead of hiding them. NIST similarly distinguishes false matches from misses under specified image and gallery conditions. ([NIST verification](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt11.html), [NIST identification](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt1N.html))

Enrollment and correction need parallel attention. Selecting the largest face can consistently enroll a friend; consistency filtering proves agreement, not ownership. Multiple-face setup should require explicit face choice. The audited uncertainty-only review missed confident false matches and unreadable back views. The patch now addresses confident picks, but every people photo should eventually offer correction, including image-specific confirmation when a face is invisible. Keep exact photo confirmations separate from transferable facial references, preserve model/version provenance, support undo, and validate references on load. Local diagnostic traces should explain detection status, readable face count, positive and negative similarity, decision source, eligibility and suppression reason. This would distinguish “recognized the wrong person” from “recognized correctly but selected the wrong subject.” ([Enrollment](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/UI/Onboarding/FaceScanView.swift), [feedback collection](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/App/RunViewModel.swift), [reference storage](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Services/Account/FaceReferenceStore.swift))

The accompanying patch retains profile-aware results when no match exists, removes automatic run references, preserves uncertainty in partially readable groups, and rejects malformed cosine inputs. It also removes framing-derived “You” labels when no profile is enrolled: unidentified people remain “Someone.” Judge category protections apply with or without enrollment; the judge cannot overwrite an existing people classification, and any newly proposed people category becomes “Someone.” The Me mix filters to `withMe == true`, including locked items, and returns a shortfall instead of substituting other subjects. After judge scoring it promotes the best eligible member of each cluster, preventing a higher-quality ineligible winner from hiding an available owner-containing frame. Zoom crops retain the original target rectangle; a new detection must be the only candidate meeting an intersection-over-union threshold of 0.3. That geometric threshold is provisional and requires real-photo validation. Feature schema 5 invalidates prior cached extractions. These are implemented changes, not proof that a model-positive match is always the owner.

Confident decisions are now correctable: `PhotoScore.matchedFace` preserves the face associated with an accepted match, Results offers “Check who is in these photos,” and the review sheet includes those confident picks alongside uncertain faces. A “Not me” answer persists as negative face evidence and is applied when selection reruns. This closes the previous inability to dispute confident selected matches. **Photo-specific confirmation of back views is not implemented**; neither is a general correction interface for every rejected or unreadable photo. ([Stored match](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/Core/Models.swift), [review collection](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/App/RunViewModel.swift), [Results control](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/UI/ResultsView.swift), [review sheet](/Users/abi/Desktop/Eymra/Sources/Eymra/UI/FaceCheck.swift))

**Simulator validation completed: 133 tests reported, 130 passed, three expected Vision issues, zero unexpected failures and zero skipped tests.** The run included the Simulator build, all `EymraTests`, and the UI tests `testConfidentMatchesCanBeReviewed`, `testIsThisYou` and `testSetupControls`. The three expected issues concern unavailable Vision feature-print, face-landmark and small-face capabilities on the Simulator; they are not successful recognition evaluations. `git diff --check` was clean. No device installation, device end-to-end extraction validation, or evaluation of the owner's actual failing photos was performed, so no measured improvement on that library is claimed.

Broader correction coverage, target-aware crop scoring, contextual back-view linking, model replacement and deployment-specific calibration remain further work. Passing regressions establish that the tested corrected paths obey their contracts. Reliable personal recognition additionally requires held-out real-library evidence that the current project lacks.
